Across 72 test-retest pairs from 26 athletes, change in leg lean mass was the stable positive predictor of change in jump concentric impulse (beta = 2.48, 95% CI 1.32 to 3.64 in the primary model), while change in surface-EMG peak amplitude was unstable and not significant. Lean mass was also the more repeatable measurement between tests (ICC 0.85, against 0.51 for EMG amplitude). A ratio of the two turned out to be driven mostly by arithmetic coupling between its numerator and denominator, so surface-EMG peak amplitude alone should not be read as a longitudinal measure of neural adaptation.
