method: "Nonlinear movement complexity measures"
---

In 28,307 fastballs from 1,722 pitchers, the average nonlinear complexity of trunk and pelvis motion was roughly flat across velocity tiers. What scaled with velocity was how consistent that complexity was from pitch to pitch within a session: on the leading measure, pitchers at 90 mph and above varied 57% less than those below 85 mph (Cohen's d = 0.61). The signal was strongest at the pelvis and trunk rather than the arm, was moderately stable across sessions (ICC about 0.52), and cannot be seen from a single pitch.
